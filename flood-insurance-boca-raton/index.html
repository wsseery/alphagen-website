Flood Insurance in Boca Raton: Flood Zones, the City’s CRS Discount and Your Options

What the City of Boca Raton and FEMA publish about local flood zones, the City’s flood-insurance discount, and how a federal NFIP policy differs from a private flood policy, so you know which questions to ask before you buy.

AlphaGen Insurance Agency is an independent Boca Raton agency (FL lic. G164863).

Is your Boca Raton property in a flood zone?

The City of Boca Raton says that a large portion of the city lies in the Special Flood Hazard Area, which it defines as the area with “a 1-percent or greater chance of flooding in any given year” (City of Boca Raton, Know Your Flood Zone).

On FEMA’s flood maps, these high-risk areas are the zones beginning with the letters A or V. Moderate- to low-risk areas are shown as zones beginning with B, C or X. FEMA describes the high-risk threshold another way: at least a 1 in 4 chance of flooding during a 30-year mortgage (FEMA, Flood Maps and Zones Explained).

The maps changed recently. The City notes that FEMA adopted new Flood Insurance Rate Maps (FIRMs) on December 20, 2024, so an older zone determination may be out of date. The City links to a FIRM lookup tool where you can enter an address. If you believe a property was wrongly placed in a high-risk zone, the City points to FEMA’s Letter of Map Amendment (LOMA) process.

Outside the high-risk zones, FEMA says “floods can happen anywhere,” and that moderate- to low-risk areas submit more than 20 percent of National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) claims.

The City of Boca Raton’s CRS flood-insurance discount

Boca Raton takes part in the NFIP’s Community Rating System (CRS), which the City describes as a voluntary incentive program. In the City’s words, “the discount increased from 15% to 25% for residents in all flood zones with qualifying NFIP policies,” and residents should contact their flood insurance provider starting in October 2025 to see when the savings apply (City of Boca Raton, Floodplain Management).

Per FEMA, communities are placed in CRS classes from 1 to 10 by the credit points they earn for floodplain management, and the premium discount steps up by 5% per class, from 5% at Class 9 to 45% at Class 1. In FEMA’s table, a 25% discount is the Class 5 step (FEMA, Community Rating System brochure, March 2023).

Two practical points:

  • It follows the City’s boundaries. Local reporting notes the discount applies to residents of the City of Boca Raton, not to everyone with a Boca Raton mailing address (WFLX). Check whether your property is inside city limits.
  • It is described for NFIP policies. The City’s notice says nothing about private flood policies; ask any private insurer how it rates your property.

NFIP vs private flood insurance

FEMA’s message to Floridians is direct: when flooding happens, “the damage is not covered by most homeowners’ insurance policies” (FEMA, Flood Insurance an Important Resource for Floridians). Flood is bought separately, either through the federal NFIP or from a private insurer.

The NFIP policy

The NFIP is federally backed. Its published limits, per FEMA’s FloodSmart site, are up to $250,000 for a residential building and up to $100,000 for contents; for non-residential property, building and contents are each available up to $500,000. Coverage generally starts 30 days after purchase. There is no wait when the policy is bought in connection with making, increasing, extending or renewing a mortgage, and a one-day wait applies in a newly designated high-risk zone if the policy is bought within 12 months of the map change. FloodSmart lists personal property kept in basements, and temporary housing or additional living expenses during repairs, as not included (FloodSmart, Buy a Flood Insurance Policy).

Private flood policies

FEMA notes that flood insurance is also available from some private insurers. Florida law sorts private flood policies into types (Fla. Stat. §627.715):

  • Standard — matches the coverage, deductibles and loss adjustment of an NFIP standard policy.
  • Preferred — the standard form with a broader definition of flood, additional living expenses, and contents valued at replacement cost up to the policy limits.
  • Customized — coverage broader than the standard form.
  • Flexible — built from specified options, such as a set coverage amount, a deductible or actual cash value adjustment.

The same statute provides that an insurer may ask Florida’s Office of Insurance Regulation to certify that its flood coverage equals or exceeds the NFIP’s. It also states that Citizens Property Insurance Corporation may not provide insurance for the peril of flood, so a home insured with Citizens needs flood from somewhere else.

If you have a mortgage, the lender has a say. FEMA says home and business owners in high-risk areas with mortgages from federally regulated or insured lenders are required to buy flood insurance. If you are weighing a private policy, confirm your lender will accept it before you change anything.

Neither route is better in the abstract. What matters is the limits, waiting period, exclusions and premium for your building, and those depend on underwriting and on the policy actually issued.

Questions to answer before you buy

  • Do you have an elevation certificate? The City keeps copies for new or substantially improved structures in Special Flood Hazard Areas.
  • Is the property inside City of Boca Raton limits, where the CRS discount applies to qualifying NFIP policies?
  • Do you need building limits, contents limits, or both, and do the NFIP limits fit your home?
  • When does coverage need to be in force, given the waiting period and the hurricane season?
