Commercial umbrella insurance in Florida

A commercial umbrella adds liability limits above your general liability, auto liability and employer's liability policies. It is usually bought because a contract, lease or lender asks for higher limits than the underlying policies carry.

Who it's for

Businesses whose contracts require higher liability limits than a primary policy provides.

Businesses with vehicles, public foot traffic or larger operations, where one serious claim could exceed the primary limit.

What it can include

  • Excess limits — above the scheduled underlying policies once their limits are used.
  • Drop-down coverage — on some umbrella forms, for certain claims the underlying policies do not cover.
  • Defense — terms vary by form.

Coverage depends on the policy. Nothing on this page describes what a specific policy will do: eligibility, underwriting and the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy actually issued decide that.

What insurers ask for

To quote this line, an underwriter needs a picture of the risk. These are the items most insurers ask for. Not every insurer asks for every item, and some ask for more.

Business basics

  • Legal name, entity type and FEIN
  • Years in business and a description of operations
  • Locations and, where relevant, the class code that best describes the business — the class code sets which rating base and rate an underwriter uses

Underlying policies

  • A schedule of the policies the umbrella sits over: general liability, auto liability and employer's liability, with insurers, limits and dates — the umbrella only starts where those limits end, so any gap becomes your own cost
  • Declarations pages for each underlying policy

Exposure

  • Number of vehicles and drivers
  • Any hazardous operations

History

  • Loss runs for every underlying line

Application forms: most insurers start from the industry-standard ACORD 125 Commercial Insurance Application and ACORD 131 Umbrella/Excess Section; many also use their own application or supplement.

Having your loss runs, inspections and current declarations ready usually speeds up a quote, because the underwriter can price from documents instead of waiting on follow-up questions.

Questions we hear

What is the difference between an umbrella and an excess policy?

An excess policy usually follows the terms of the policy beneath it. An umbrella can be broader, sometimes covering claims the underlying policies do not. The wording decides.

Do I need specific underlying limits?

Yes. The umbrella insurer will require minimum limits on the underlying policies and will ask for their declarations pages.

Does an umbrella cover professional liability?

Usually not. Professional liability is normally excluded and handled on its own policy.

How much does a commercial umbrella cost in Florida?

What drives the price is the business itself: what it does, its revenue or payroll, its location, its claims history, the limits and deductibles chosen, and the insurer's appetite for that class of business.
