Multifamily and HOA insurance in Broward County

Apartment buildings, condominium associations and homeowner associations in Broward County do not buy insurance the way a storefront does. The building is the business. The most expensive components are mechanical. The people who pay for an uninsured loss are unit owners, by assessment, and they find out at a board meeting.

We work with multifamily owners, management companies and association boards from Fort Lauderdale to Pompano Beach, Coral Springs, Plantation, Hollywood and Weston — reading the program as a whole rather than quoting one line at a time.

What a multifamily or association program usually has to answer

Commercial property. The building, its common elements and the schedule of what is actually insured, with two deductibles that do different jobs: one for hurricane or windstorm losses, one for everything else. Both are stated on the declarations page as specific dollar amounts.

Equipment breakdown. Chillers, boilers, pumps, elevator machinery, booster systems. Most commercial property forms are written around perils — things that happen to a building — and a machine that fails on its own is a different question. Where equipment breakdown is endorsed onto the policy, the schedule of covered equipment and the extra-expense limit behind it are the parts worth reading.

General liability. Common areas, walkways, pool decks, parking, and the contractors who work on site. The certificate requirements in your vendor contracts belong in the same conversation.

Directors and officers. Volunteer board members make decisions about money, rules and enforcement. Most association D&O forms are written for exactly that exposure, and the definition of who counts as an insured person is the part most boards have never read.

Where the association’s policy stops and a unit owner’s begins. Governing documents, not the insurance policy, usually draw that line first. The two documents have to be read together, and a master policy is not a substitute for a unit owner’s own coverage.

Coverage in every one of those lines depends on the actual terms of the policy issued. Nothing on this page states that a policy covers a specific loss — your own exclusions page is where that is settled.

What we do differently on a habitational account

We start with the documents you already have: the current declarations pages, the schedule of forms and endorsements, the equipment list, and the governing documents’ insurance article. Most of what a board needs to decide is already written down in those four places and has simply never been read side by side.

Then we tell you what we find before we quote anything — including the parts that are already right. A renewal is worth moving only when there is a reason, and a board deserves to hear the reason in plain terms.
