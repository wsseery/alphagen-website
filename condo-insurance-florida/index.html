Condo insurance (HO-6) in Florida

A condo unit-owner policy, usually called an HO-6, insures what the association's master policy does not: your belongings, the parts of the unit your declaration makes your responsibility, your liability, and often loss assessments.

AlphaGen Insurance Agency is an independent agency in Boca Raton, licensed in Florida, that compares several insurers for Florida businesses and households.

Who it's for

Owners of a condominium unit they live in, and owners who rent their unit out (who need a policy written for rentals).

Buyers whose lender requires an HO-6 before closing.

What it can include

  • Unit improvements and fixtures — the parts of the unit your condo documents make the owner's responsibility.
  • Personal property — your belongings inside the unit.
  • Loss assessment — your share of a special assessment after a covered loss, on many forms.
  • Personal liability and loss of use.

Coverage depends on the policy. Nothing on this page describes what a specific policy will do: eligibility, underwriting and the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy actually issued decide that.

What insurers ask for

To quote this line, an underwriter needs a picture of the risk. These are the items most insurers ask for. Not every insurer asks for every item, and some ask for more.

The association

  • The association's master-policy summary: what it insures inside the unit and its deductibles — the master policy decides where your unit coverage has to start

The unit

  • Improvements and upgrades you have made
  • The floor the unit is on, the building's year built and its updates
  • The loss-assessment limit you want
  • Whether you live in the unit or rent it out

Florida documents

  • A wind-mitigation report for the building, if the association has one
  • Your flood zone

History

  • Claims in the past five years and your current insurer

Application forms: most insurers start from the industry-standard ACORD 80 Homeowner Application; many also use their own application or supplement.

Having your loss runs, inspections and current declarations ready usually speeds up a quote, because the underwriter can price from documents instead of waiting on follow-up questions.

Questions we hear

Doesn't the association insure my unit?

Partly. Florida's condominium law divides what the association insures from what the unit owner insures, and your declaration fills in the details. The HO-6 covers your side of that line.

What documents will the insurer ask for?

Often your declarations page from the master policy or the association's insurance summary, and details of any upgrades you made to the unit.

Do I need flood insurance in a condo?

Possibly. The master policy may or may not carry flood, and it will not cover your belongings. Ask for a separate unit-owner flood quote.
