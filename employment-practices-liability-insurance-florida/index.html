Employment practices liability insurance (EPLI) in Florida

Employment practices liability insurance responds to claims by employees, former employees and job applicants alleging wrongful employment acts, such as discrimination, harassment, wrongful termination and retaliation. General liability policies commonly exclude these claims, and workers' compensation does not address them.

Who it's for

Any business with employees. Claims come from hiring, discipline, promotion and termination decisions, not only at large employers.

Businesses with high turnover, recent layoffs, or no dedicated human-resources staff.

Businesses whose employees deal with the public, which may want coverage for harassment claims brought by customers or other third parties.

What it can include

  • Employment claims — discrimination, harassment, wrongful termination, retaliation and similar allegations.
  • Defense costs — on many forms these are paid inside the limit, so they reduce what is left for a settlement.
  • Third-party coverage — claims by customers or vendors, where the insurer offers it as an option.
  • Claims-made terms — the policy responds to claims first made while it is in force, subject to a retroactive date.

Coverage depends on the policy. Nothing on this page describes what a specific policy will do: eligibility, underwriting and the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy actually issued decide that.

What insurers ask for

To quote this line, an underwriter needs a picture of the risk. These are the items most insurers ask for. Not every insurer asks for every item, and some ask for more.

Business basics

  • Legal name, entity type and FEIN
  • Years in business and a description of operations
  • Locations and, where relevant, the class code that best describes the business — the class code sets which rating base and rate an underwriter uses

Employees

  • Number of full-time, part-time and seasonal employees and independent contractors, and where they work
  • Turnover and terminations in the past year, and any layoffs made or planned — terminations and layoffs are where most employment claims begin

Employment practices

  • An employee handbook with anti-harassment, complaint and discipline procedures
  • Who handles human resources: your own staff, an outside firm or a professional employer organization
  • Training for managers and staff

History

  • Past charges with the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission or the state, demand letters and lawsuits, and any known circumstances
  • Your current EPLI insurer and retroactive date, if any

Application forms: most insurers start from the industry-standard ACORD 827 Employment Practices Liability Insurance Section; most insurers also use their own application; many also use their own application or supplement.

Having your loss runs, inspections and current declarations ready usually speeds up a quote, because the underwriter can price from documents instead of waiting on follow-up questions.

Questions we hear

Isn't this covered by my general liability policy?

Usually not. General liability policies commonly exclude employment-related claims, which is why EPLI is a separate policy or endorsement.

What does claims-made mean?

The policy responds to claims first made during the policy period, for acts after its retroactive date. Keeping coverage continuous protects that retroactive date when you renew or change insurers.

Are wage-and-hour claims covered?

Usually not. Most EPLI policies exclude wage-and-hour claims, though some offer a limited amount for defense. Check the specific policy.

How much does EPLI cost in Florida?

What drives the price is the business itself: the number of employees and where they work, turnover and recent layoffs, your written employment practices, your claims history, and the limits and retention chosen.
