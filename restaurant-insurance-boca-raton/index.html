Restaurant insurance in Boca Raton

Palm Beach County restaurants carry four different insurance problems and usually buy one policy. This page is about the seams between them — the places a Boca Raton or Delray operator finds out what they did not buy.

AlphaGen is an independent agency in Boca Raton, licensed in Florida, writing commercial lines for Main-Street operators across Palm Beach and Broward.

The three risks that show up first

1. The wet floor

A tray goes down by the pass; someone steps in it minutes later. Under Florida law the injured person must prove the business had actual or constructive knowledge of the substance — and the statute allows constructive knowledge to be proven by showing the condition “occurred with regularity and was therefore foreseeable” (Fla. Stat. §768.0755).

The practical consequence is not an insurance one: a sweep log that records the same hazard repeatedly, and shows nothing done about it, is evidence against you. Housekeeping discipline is part of your risk profile, and underwriters price it.

2. The bar

Florida limits the liability of a business that serves alcohol to an adult, but leaves two doors open — willful and unlawful service to someone under the legal drinking age, and knowingly serving a person habitually addicted (Fla. Stat. §768.125).

Both turn on what your staff knew and what you can show you trained them to do. And most general liability forms exclude liquor liability precisely for businesses in the business of serving it — so if you hold a licence, the exposure and the policy you bought are usually two separate things. Check your own forms.

3. The food

The CDC’s 2019 estimates put domestically acquired foodborne illness from seven major pathogens at roughly 53,300 hospitalizations and 931 deaths a year, with about 9.9 million illnesses attributed to six of them (CDC, Burden of Foodborne Illness).

A claim of this kind is normally evaluated under the products-completed operations part of a general liability policy. Whether that part carries its own aggregate or shares the general aggregate is one of the most consequential lines in the policy and one of the least-read.

What a commercial policy is built to address

Written plainly, with the caveat attached to each.

  • General liability (GL) — third-party bodily injury and property damage arising from your premises, operations and product. Most forms also fund the defence of a covered claim.
  • Property — the building, contents and equipment. Spoilage and equipment breakdown are frequently not included by default and are reached by endorsement.
  • Workers’ compensation — injuries to your own employees. Not a general liability matter, and required in Florida at defined employee thresholds.
  • Liquor liability — normally a separate policy for licensed establishments, because the GL form typically excludes it.
  • Business interruption — lost income while you cannot trade. This is the one that matters most from June to November.

None of the above describes what any specific policy will do. Coverage depends on eligibility, underwriting and the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy actually issued to you. The point of a review is to read yours.

Why this page exists

AlphaGen is on Glades Road in Boca Raton — the same corridor as a large share of the restaurants this page is written for. Late-night alcohol service is more common here than the statewide picture suggests, which pushes the liquor and assault-and-battery questions to the front of the list for local operators, and hurricane-season business interruption sits under all of it for half the year.

That combination is why a Boca Raton restaurant’s insurance problem is rarely one policy. It is the seam between four.
