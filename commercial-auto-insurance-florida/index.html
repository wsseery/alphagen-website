Commercial auto insurance in Florida

Commercial auto insurance covers the vehicles your business owns, leases or uses: liability for injuries and damage you cause to others and, if you choose, damage to the vehicles themselves. A personal auto policy often will not respond when a vehicle is titled to the business or used mainly for work.

Who it's for

Businesses that own or lease cars, vans, pickups or box trucks: contractors, delivery and service businesses, landscapers, caterers and sales teams.

Businesses with no vehicles of their own whose employees drive their own or rented cars on business, which is what hired and non-owned auto coverage is for.

Florida's no-fault law requires most vehicles registered in the state to carry personal injury protection (Florida Statutes section 627.733), so a Florida business vehicle needs it too.

What it can include

  • Auto liability — bodily injury and property damage to others arising from your business vehicles.
  • Personal injury protection (PIP) — the no-fault medical and wage benefits Florida law requires.
  • Uninsured and underinsured motorist — injuries caused by a driver with too little or no insurance, where you choose it.
  • Physical damage — comprehensive and collision coverage for the vehicles themselves.
  • Hired and non-owned auto — liability when employees use rented vehicles or their own cars on business.

Coverage depends on the policy. Nothing on this page describes what a specific policy will do: eligibility, underwriting and the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy actually issued decide that.

What insurers ask for

To quote this line, an underwriter needs a picture of the risk. These are the items most insurers ask for. Not every insurer asks for every item, and some ask for more.

Business basics

  • Legal name, entity type and FEIN
  • Years in business and a description of operations
  • Locations and, where relevant, the class code that best describes the business — the class code sets which rating base and rate an underwriter uses

Vehicles

  • A vehicle schedule: year, make, model, VIN, cost new or stated value, and where each vehicle is garaged — the garaging address and the type of vehicle set much of the base rate
  • How each vehicle is used and how far it usually travels from its base
  • Any lienholders or lessors to be named on the policy

Drivers

  • A driver list with license numbers, dates of birth and years of driving experience — insurers order a motor vehicle record for every listed driver
  • Your hiring standards and how often you check drivers' records
  • Any fleet safety program, dash cameras or telematics

History

  • Three to five years of currently valued loss runs from your current and prior insurers — they show claims the insurer would otherwise only learn about later
  • Your current insurer, current premium and expiration date

Application forms: most insurers start from the industry-standard ACORD 125 Commercial Insurance Application, ACORD 127 Business Auto Section and ACORD 129 Vehicle Schedule, with the Florida ACORD 137 FL state section; some also use the ACORD 163 Commercial Auto Driver Information Schedule; many also use their own application or supplement.

Having your loss runs, inspections and current declarations ready usually speeds up a quote, because the underwriter can price from documents instead of waiting on follow-up questions.

Questions we hear

Can I use my personal auto policy for my work truck?

Often not. When a vehicle is titled to the business or used mainly for business, many personal policies limit or exclude coverage, and insurers generally expect a commercial auto policy instead. Tell the insurer exactly how the vehicle is used.

What is hired and non-owned auto coverage?

Liability coverage for your business when an employee drives a rented vehicle, or their own car, on company business. It protects the business; it does not repair the employee's car.

Do I have to list every driver?

Most insurers ask for a driver list and check each driver's motor vehicle record. A driver with serious violations can change the price or be excluded from the policy.

How much does commercial auto insurance cost in Florida?

What drives the price is the vehicles and the people driving them: the type and value of each vehicle, how it is used and how far it travels, where it is garaged, the drivers' records, the business's claims history, and the limits and deductibles chosen.
