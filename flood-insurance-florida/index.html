Private flood insurance in Florida

Flood is excluded from homeowners, condo and most commercial property policies. Private flood insurers write it as a separate policy, often with different limits, waiting periods and terms from the federal National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP).

AlphaGen Insurance Agency is an independent agency in Boca Raton, licensed in Florida, that compares several insurers for Florida businesses and households.

Who it's for

Florida homeowners, condo owners and businesses in or near a flood zone, and anyone whose lender requires flood coverage.

Owners who need higher limits or different terms than a federal flood policy offers.

What it can include

  • Building — the structure, up to the limit chosen.
  • Contents — usually a separate limit you choose.
  • Loss of use or business income — on some private forms.
  • Replacement cost — available on some private forms; terms vary widely.

Coverage depends on the policy. Nothing on this page describes what a specific policy will do: eligibility, underwriting and the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy actually issued decide that.

What insurers ask for

To quote this line, an underwriter needs a picture of the risk. These are the items most insurers ask for. Not every insurer asks for every item, and some ask for more.

Location and building

  • Flood zone
  • An elevation certificate (FEMA Form FF-206-FY-22-152) if one exists — it records the building's elevation and can change the premium considerably
  • Foundation type, height of the first floor above ground and any flood openings
  • Whether utilities such as the air handler and water heater are raised

Coverage and history

  • The building and contents limits you want
  • Prior flood claims and any current flood policy, including whether coverage has been continuous

Application forms: private flood insurers use their own applications.

Having your loss runs, inspections and current declarations ready usually speeds up a quote, because the underwriter can price from documents instead of waiting on follow-up questions.

Questions we hear

Will my lender accept a private flood policy?

Federal rules require regulated lenders to accept private flood policies that meet set criteria. Ask your lender to confirm before you switch.

What is an elevation certificate?

A FEMA form completed by a surveyor or engineer that records the building's elevation relative to expected flood levels. Many insurers use it to price flood.

Is there a waiting period?

Most flood policies have one before coverage begins, though some private policies differ. Check the specific policy, especially during hurricane season.

How much does private flood insurance cost in Florida?

What drives the price is the property itself: its location and flood zone, its age, roof and construction, its wind-mitigation features, the coverage limits and deductibles chosen, and its claims history.
