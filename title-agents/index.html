How it works

Built to take one step off your closing checklist, not to add one.

  1. Send the link. Forward it to the buyer, or drop it into the email you already send at contract. No introduction needed.
  2. They get quoted. Homeowners, and flood as a separate placement where the lender requires it. Coastal exposure and older roofs included rather than declined on sight.
  3. You get the binder. Evidence of property insurance in the form your lender expects, in time for the closing you already scheduled.

What we write

Florida property and casualty, placed through an independent agency, so the risk goes to a carrier that will actually take it.

  • Homeowners (HO-3) and condo unit-owner (HO-6). Single family, townhome and condo. See Florida homeowners and condo insurance in Florida.
  • Flood. A separate policy from homeowners. Where the lender determines the property sits in a special flood hazard area, that is a second placement with its own timeline — start it at contract, not at clear-to-close. More on Florida flood insurance.
  • Commercial property. For the investor, small-business and mixed-use side of your book. Start a commercial property quote.

Let’s be plain about the arrangement

There isn’t one. No referral fee, no revenue share, no gift, no marketing spend and no reciprocal-referral promise, in either direction. Title insurance is a settlement service, and arrangements like that are what the Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act prohibits.

What we’re offering is narrower and more useful: a number your buyer can call when they don’t have one, so your file closes on the date it was supposed to.
