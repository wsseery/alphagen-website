Professional Office Insurance in Boca Raton

A professional office carries almost no inventory and almost all of its risk in its files, its email and its advice. Accounting practices, law firms, consultancies, design studios, agencies and insurance offices across Boca Raton and Palm Beach County share the same shape: a small headcount, a client list worth protecting, and an exposure profile that looks nothing like a restaurant’s or a contractor’s. The coverage conversation follows that shape.

What a Boca Raton professional office usually needs

Cyber liability. The client data and the email account are the assets. Florida’s breach statute gives a business no later than 30 days from determining a breach to notify affected individuals, and the same 30 days to notify the Department of Legal Affairs where 500 or more Floridians are involved. (Fla. Stat. §501.171) A cyber policy is what is built to pay for the forensics, the legal read and the notification — and to respond if a regulator asks questions.

Professional liability (errors and omissions). The advice, the filing, the deliverable. General liability policies commonly exclude professional services, so this is a separate policy. It is usually claims-made, which makes the retroactive date on the declarations page worth reading before you switch carriers.

General liability. Third-party bodily injury and property damage — the reception area, the hallway, the client who trips on a cable. Not your staff, and not your own equipment.

Business personal property. Workstations, servers, the build-out. A landlord’s policy is not yours.

Workers’ compensation. For employees, once Florida’s thresholds are met.

Directors and officers, where there is a board, outside investors or a nonprofit structure.

Most professional office policies address these as separate lines rather than a single bundle, and the sublimits are where the differences live. Check your exclusions and sublimits page.

The gap we find most often

The funds-transfer-fraud sublimit. A business can carry a seven-figure cyber limit and a five-figure social-engineering sublimit, and a fraudulent wire instruction is the claim that actually arrives. The two numbers are on different pages of the same policy, and almost nobody has read the second one.

The second most common: a cyber endorsement on a business owner’s policy (BOP) being mistaken for a cyber policy. The endorsement typically carries a low “electronic data” sublimit and no breach-response team attached.
