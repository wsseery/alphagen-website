Law firm insurance in Boca Raton

Law firm insurance in Boca Raton is bought in a different order from almost every other business’s. A restaurant insures the room. A contractor insures the work site. A firm’s exposure is the engagement itself — the deadline, the conflict, the advice — and almost all of it sits in files and email rather than in anything you could photograph.

AlphaGen Insurance Agency works with small and mid-size professional practices across Palm Beach and Broward counties: solo practitioners, three-partner firms, and offices of a dozen with a paralegal team and a lease.

What a small firm is usually buying

Lawyers professional liability (legal malpractice). The policy written for the allegation that the work was done badly, or not done. It is almost always claims-made, which means what matters is when the claim is made against you — and whether the retroactive date on your declarations page reaches back far enough to include the engagement being complained about. Switching carriers and resetting that date is the most expensive avoidable mistake in this line.

General liability. The physical world: a visitor who falls in reception, damage to the suite. Usually the cheapest policy in the file and the one your lease requires.

Commercial property, or contents cover if you lease. Workstations, servers, the library, the furniture. Written at replacement cost or actual cash value — the declarations page decides which, and it changes what your limit is worth.

Cyber. A firm holds other people’s confidential information as a condition of doing business. Most cyber policies address the investigation, the notification obligations and the claims that follow; check your exclusions page. Cyber is a separate policy from professional liability, not a section of it.

Workers’ compensation, once you have employees, under Florida’s statutory definition of covered employment.

Commercial auto or hired and non-owned auto, if anyone drives for the firm — including in their own car.

Where firms most often find a gap

The retroactive date. Found on the declarations page, rarely read until it matters.

Prior acts after a merger or a name change. Two entities, one policy, and a named insured line that names only one of them.

“The landlord’s certificate covers us.” Being an additional insured on someone else’s policy is not the same as having a policy. The limits belong to the named insured and are shared with every other claim against it.

Cyber and professional liability assumed to overlap. Most cyber forms exclude liability arising out of the rendering of professional services, and plenty of professional liability forms exclude breach-response costs. Read both exclusions pages together.

The extended reporting period. On a claims-made policy, the tail has to be bought, usually within a short window after cancellation. Ask the price before you need it.

How we work

Independent, so the quote is shopped rather than fulfilled: we approach the carriers appointed for professional lines in Florida and come back with the comparison, including what each form excludes rather than only what each one costs. For a firm already insured, the most useful first step is usually a read of the policy you have — the declarations page, the endorsement list and the exclusions — against the work the firm actually does now rather than the work it did when the policy was first written.
