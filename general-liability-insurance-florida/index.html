General liability insurance in Florida

Commercial general liability (CGL) responds to claims that your business caused bodily injury or property damage to someone else, or certain personal and advertising injury. It is the policy landlords, clients and general contractors most often ask to see a certificate for.

Who it's for

Almost every business that deals with the public, works at a customer's site, or signs a contract or lease that requires proof of insurance.

Contractors, retailers, restaurants, property owners and service businesses are the most frequent buyers; the class of business shapes the price more than anything else.

What it can include

  • Premises and operations — injuries or damage at your location or caused by your work.
  • Products and completed operations — claims arising from what you sold or work you finished.
  • Personal and advertising injury — such as certain defamation or copyright claims in your advertising.
  • Defense costs — most forms fund the defense of a covered claim; whether that reduces your limit depends on the form.

Coverage depends on the policy. Nothing on this page describes what a specific policy will do: eligibility, underwriting and the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy actually issued decide that.

What insurers ask for

To quote this line, an underwriter needs a picture of the risk. These are the items most insurers ask for. Not every insurer asks for every item, and some ask for more.

Business basics

  • Legal name, entity type and FEIN
  • Years in business and a description of operations
  • Locations and, where relevant, the class code that best describes the business — the class code sets which rating base and rate an underwriter uses

Exposure

  • Annual revenue or payroll, depending on the class of business
  • The cost of work you subcontract

Line-specific

  • Products you make or sell and work you complete for others
  • Contracts you sign that require additional insured status or specific limits — contracts can shift someone else's liability onto your policy
  • Certificates of insurance from your subcontractors and vendors
  • Any safety program

History

  • Three to five years of currently valued loss runs from your current and prior insurers — they show claims the insurer would otherwise only learn about later
  • Your current insurer, current premium and expiration date

Application forms: most insurers start from the industry-standard ACORD 125 Commercial Insurance Application and ACORD 126 Commercial General Liability Section; many also use their own application or supplement.

Having your loss runs, inspections and current declarations ready usually speeds up a quote, because the underwriter can price from documents instead of waiting on follow-up questions.

Questions we hear

Is general liability required in Florida?

Florida law does not require every business to carry it, but many leases, contracts, licensing bodies and general contractors require proof of it before you can start work.

Does general liability cover my employees' injuries?

No. Injuries to your own employees are a workers' compensation matter, not a general liability matter.

What is a certificate of insurance?

A one-page summary, usually on a standard ACORD certificate form, that shows a third party which policies you carry and their limits. It does not change the policy itself.

How much does general liability cost in Florida?

What drives the price is the business itself: what it does, its revenue or payroll, its location, its claims history, the limits and deductibles chosen, and the insurer's appetite for that class of business.
