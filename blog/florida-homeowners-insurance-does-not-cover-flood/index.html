Florida Homeowners Insurance Does Not Cover Flood

Florida homeowners insurance does not cover flood under a standard policy, and that is the most common gap on a Florida home. It is not a loophole. It is how the policies are built: a standard homeowners policy is generally written to exclude flood, so flood is a separate purchase.

Your homeowners policy is not your flood policy

Flood coverage is bought through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) or from a private flood carrier. FEMA puts it plainly for Floridians: when flooding happens, “the damage is not covered by most homeowners’ insurance policies” (FEMA, Flood Insurance an Important Resource for Floridians). Whether a particular policy responds to a particular loss depends on its wording, so read yours or ask your agent.

Flood and water damage are not the same thing

A pipe that bursts inside the house and water that rises into the house from outside are handled by different coverage. Which one applies turns on where the water came from, not on how wet the floor is. Two homeowners can stand in the same wet living room and be dealing with two different policies.

That is worth knowing before a claim, because the question gets asked after the damage, when the answer is already fixed by the policies you bought.

Flood coverage does not start the day you buy it

FEMA states there is typically a 30-day waiting period from the date of purchase before a new flood policy takes effect, with some exceptions (FloodSmart, Buy a Flood Insurance Policy). The week a storm gets a name is the wrong week to start. If you are thinking about flood coverage, the useful time to ask is well before the forecast makes the decision for you.

Outside a high-risk zone is not outside the question

If your home sits outside a mapped high-risk zone, that puts you outside the mandatory-purchase requirement. It does not put you outside the question. A requirement tells you whether a lender will insist on flood coverage. It does not tell you whether your own home can take on water. For how flood zones are mapped and how NFIP and private flood policies differ, see our page on flood insurance in Boca Raton.

Questions to ask about your own home

  1. Does my homeowners policy exclude flood? Look for the exclusion in the policy wording, or ask your agent to point to it.
  2. Do I have a separate flood policy? If so, is it NFIP or private, and when did it take effect?
  3. Which coverage would apply to water from inside versus water from outside? Ask how your policies treat each, using the source of the water as the starting point.
  4. Where is my home on the flood map? A mapped zone affects whether purchase is mandatory, not whether flooding is possible.

Nothing here states what any policy will pay. Coverage is determined by the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy issued to you.
