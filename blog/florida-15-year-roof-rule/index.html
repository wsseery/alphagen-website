Florida 15-Year Roof Rule: What Fla. Stat. 627.7011 Says About Roof Age

The Florida 15-year roof rule is a short piece of Fla. Stat. §627.7011 that is worth reading before you accept that your roof is the problem. It speaks to roof age, and the word that matters most in it is “solely.”

What the statute says about roofs under 15 years old

Under Fla. Stat. §627.7011, an insurer may not refuse to issue or refuse to renew a homeowner’s policy insuring a residential structure with a roof that is less than 15 years old solely because of the age of the roof.

Solely is the operative word. Condition is a different conversation from age, and so is a roof that has failed an inspection. But age alone, under 15 years, is not a reason on its own.

What it says about roofs that are 15 years or older

For a roof that is at least 15 years old, the same statute requires the insurer to allow the homeowner to have a roof inspection performed by an authorized inspector, at the homeowner’s expense, before requiring the roof to be replaced. If that inspection indicates the roof has five or more years of useful life remaining, the statute addresses what the insurer may do from there.

Who counts as an authorized inspector

The statute lists them:

  • a home inspector licensed under s. 468.8314;
  • a building code inspector certified under s. 468.607;
  • a general, building or residential contractor licensed under s. 489.111, or a roofing contractor;
  • a professional engineer licensed under s. 471.015;
  • a professional architect licensed under s. 481.213; or
  • another individual or entity the insurer recognizes as qualified.

Know the age before renewal season

Know your roof’s age before renewal season, not during it. The roof’s age is the first fact the statute turns on.
