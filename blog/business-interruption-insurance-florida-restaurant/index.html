Business interruption insurance for a Florida restaurant: what can you claim after a hurricane?

When a hurricane closes a restaurant, the question is not how bad the storm was. It is what the policy says. Business interruption insurance for a Florida restaurant is written around wording: what triggers it, what it excludes, and when the clock starts. The answer is in the wording, not the weather.

What business interruption generally does

Business interruption is generally tied to direct physical damage to your property from a covered peril. Where it applies, it is written to replace lost net income, and it may help with continuing costs such as rent and payroll while repairs are underway (NAIC).

That description has two parts worth reading twice. The first is the trigger: physical damage from a covered peril. The second is what it replaces: net income and certain continuing costs, not everything a closed kitchen loses. Whether any of it responds to your closure depends on the terms of the policy actually issued to you.

Three places to look before the next storm

Flood

The NAIC notes business interruption does not typically cover flood losses. The federal flood program does not fill that gap either: FEMA’s NFIP commercial policies offer up to $500,000 each for building and contents, and list financial losses from business interruption as not covered (FEMA FloodSmart). So a restaurant closed by rising water should not assume either policy replaces lost income. Our page on flood insurance in Boca Raton covers the flood side.

The closed road

If authorities block access but your building is undamaged, the question moves to a civil authority clause. The NAIC notes these generally require access to be completely prohibited and physical damage nearby from a covered peril, and that they usually have time limits and waiting periods. Read the clause itself.

The clock

Check whether your business income coverage starts the moment you close or after a waiting period, and how long that period is. Find the answer in your policy before the season, not after the closure.

What to check this week

Pull out the property policy, not the certificate, and find three things: the business income section, any flood exclusion, and the civil authority clause with its waiting period and time limit. Write down what you find. For the liability side, see what a restaurant’s general liability policy actually excludes, and for the wider picture, restaurant insurance in Boca Raton.
