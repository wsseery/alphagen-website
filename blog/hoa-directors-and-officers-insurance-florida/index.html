HOA Directors and Officers Insurance Florida: What the Fidelity Bond Does Not Do

HOA directors and officers insurance in Florida is a different thing from the fidelity bond the law already requires, and the two answer different questions. One is about money going missing. The other is about a board decision being challenged.

What Florida law already requires

Fla. Stat. 718.111(11)(h) says: “The association shall maintain insurance or fidelity bonding of all persons who control or disburse funds of the association.”

That bond answers the question of money going missing. It does not answer the question of a decision being challenged.

What the statute says about board decisions

The statute is explicit that decisions are owed a duty. “The officers and directors of the association have a fiduciary relationship to the unit owners” (§718.111(1)(a)). They must discharge their duties “in good faith” with “the care an ordinarily prudent person in a like position would exercise under similar circumstances” (§718.111(1)(d)).

Where personal liability comes in

That same subsection points to s. 617.0834 for personal liability: a breach that violates criminal law, produces an improper personal benefit, or “constitutes recklessness or an act or omission that was in bad faith.”

Where directors and officers liability fits

Directors and officers liability is the line written for the defence of those decisions. What any particular policy covers is a question for its own terms and exclusions, so read them rather than assuming. Our page on directors and officers insurance in Florida covers the line itself, and our page on multifamily and HOA insurance in Broward County covers the wider association program.

What to check

  • Whether your association carries D&O at all.
  • Whether it is separate from the fidelity bond.
