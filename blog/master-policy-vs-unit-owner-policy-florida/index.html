Master Policy vs Unit Owner Policy Florida: What Fla. Stat. 718.111(11)(f) Says

Master policy vs unit owner policy in Florida is the question behind “the association covers the building,” a sentence that is true and almost useless. Florida law is more specific than that, and the specifics are where the uninsured loss lives.

What the association’s policy must cover

Fla. Stat. 718.111(11)(f) requires the association’s policy to provide primary coverage for:

  • “All portions of the condominium property as originally installed or replacement of like kind and quality,” plus alterations or additions made under s. 718.113(2).

What the statute expressly excludes

The same subsection then excludes, expressly:

  • “All personal property within the unit or limited common elements.”
  • “Floor, wall, and ceiling coverings, electrical fixtures, appliances, water heaters, water filters, built-in cabinets and countertops, and window treatments, including curtains, drapes, blinds, hardware.”

And the statute attaches its own conclusion: “Such property and any insurance thereupon is the responsibility of the unit owner.”

What is not on the exclusion list

Air conditioning and heating equipment is not among the items enumerated in (11)(f). Where it sits depends on the declaration and the policy, which is exactly why that pair of documents gets read together rather than separately.

Why the two documents get read together

The association’s policy and the unit owner’s policy are written for different property. The statute draws the line between them, and the line is more specific than “the building” versus “the contents.” Items such as floor, wall and ceiling coverings, built-in cabinets and countertops, and window treatments are named on the owner’s side. A unit owner who assumes the master policy handles them can find the gap only after a loss.

That is why the statute’s language is worth reading directly, then comparing against your declaration and your own policy. The three documents together show where the association’s responsibility ends and yours begins.

The useful question for unit owners

When did you last price the replacement cost of that list? What to check is whether your HO-6 policy covers that list, at today’s replacement cost, and to read it alongside your declaration. Whether it does depends on your policy’s actual terms, so read them rather than assuming.
