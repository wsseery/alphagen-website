Replacement Cost vs Actual Cash Value: The Line That Decides Your Claim

Replacement cost vs actual cash value: which one is on your restaurant’s property policy? That one line can decide what a claim pays, and it is worth finding before hurricane season ends.

Two ways a policy can value a loss

The NAIC explains the difference between the two valuation methods in its consumer article, “What’s the Difference Between Actual Cash Value Coverage and Replacement Cost Coverage?”

  • Actual cash value pays to repair or replace property based on its value, considering its age and wear and tear (depreciation).
  • Replacement cost pays to repair or replace it using materials of like kind and quality.

The NAIC’s $10,000 example

The NAIC’s example uses $10,000 of damage. With replacement cost, the policy pays the $10,000 repair, minus the deductible. With actual cash value, age and condition are considered, so the payout is lower. That is the NAIC’s illustration of how the method works, not a statement about any real policy or claim.

Why it matters for a restaurant

A restaurant has more than one kind of property: the building, if you own it, and the equipment and inventory inside. The valuation method is set in the policy, and it may not be the same for each of them. Coverage depends on your policy’s actual terms, which is why the line is worth reading rather than assuming.

What to check before hurricane season ends

  • Find the valuation line for your building and read whether it says replacement cost or actual cash value.
  • Find the valuation line for your equipment and inventory, and check it separately.
  • Keep your inventory list current, so it reflects what you actually have.

The valuation line is one of several places where a commercial property policy’s wording matters. Our post on debris removal coverage covers another, and what a restaurant’s general liability policy excludes covers the liability side. Which one is on your policy?
