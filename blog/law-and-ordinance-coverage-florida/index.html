Law and Ordinance Coverage in Florida: What Fla. Stat. 627.7011 Says

Law and ordinance coverage in Florida pays for something a homeowners claim does not always make obvious: the cost of meeting today’s building rules when a damaged home is repaired. Florida addresses it directly in Fla. Stat. 627.7011.

Putting it back is not always what the building department allows

A homeowners claim is usually framed as putting the house back the way it was. After a serious loss, that is often not what the building department will allow. The repair may have to meet today’s code, and the cost of meeting it is a separate line from the cost of the repair itself.

What the statute requires an insurer to offer

Under Fla. Stat. 627.7011, before issuing a homeowners policy, an insurer must offer coverage that includes the costs necessary to meet applicable laws and ordinances regulating the construction, use or repair of property, including the costs of removing debris. The statute allows those additional costs to be limited to 25 percent or 50 percent of the dwelling limit, as selected by the policyholder.

Three details worth reading slowly

The default. Unless the insurer obtains the policyholder’s written refusal, a policy covering the dwelling is deemed to include law and ordinance coverage limited to 25 percent of the dwelling limit.

The damaged portion. The coverage applies only to repairs of the damaged portion of the structure, unless the total damage exceeds 50 percent of the structure’s replacement cost. A partial loss and a near-total loss are treated differently.

The reminder. The insurer must provide notice of the availability of the coverage at least once every 3 years. It is an easy page to skim past at renewal.

The useful question

The useful question is not only whether you have law and ordinance coverage, since Florida’s default is 25 percent unless it was refused in writing. It is which percentage you have, and whether you chose it.

Your declarations page and policy forms are where to look. Our post on how to read a declarations page walks through where limits and endorsements appear. How law and ordinance coverage sits alongside the dwelling limit depends on the policy form, so read the actual terms rather than assuming.
