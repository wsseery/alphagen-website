Florida Wind Mitigation Inspection Form: The Statute, the Form, and the Date on It

The Florida wind mitigation inspection form is the document that connects a particular house to the windstorm credits built into homeowners insurance pricing. Florida has written those credits into the way homeowners insurance is priced. The paperwork that ties your house to them is much easier to lose track of.

This post walks through the statute, the form and the one detail on it worth checking. It is general education, not a reading of anyone’s policy.

The statute: Fla. Stat. 627.0629

Under Fla. Stat. 627.0629(1), a rate filing for residential property insurance “must include actuarially reasonable discounts, credits, or other rate differentials, or appropriate reductions in deductibles” for properties with fixtures or construction techniques demonstrated to reduce the amount of loss in a windstorm. The list the statute gives includes roof strength, roof covering performance, roof-to-wall strength, opening protection, and window, door and skylight strength.

“Effective October 1, 2023,” the same section also requires insurers to describe the hurricane mitigation discounts available to policyholders on their websites.

The form: OIR-B1-1802

What turns those features into something an insurer can rate is documentation. The Office of Insurance Regulation’s form for it is the Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form, OIR-B1-1802. According to the Florida OIR wind mitigation resources page, the form notes: an “authorized inspector completes this form during wind mitigation inspection.” Two#8220;Authorized inspector completes this form during wind mitigation inspection.an “authorized inspector completes this form during wind mitigation inspection.” Two#8221; Two points from OIR worth knowing this year:

  • The form “has been updated effective April 1, 2026.”
  • It is “valid for up to five (5) years provided no material changes are made to the structure or inaccuracies are found on the form.”

The date on it

The second point is the practical one. A new roof, impact windows or shutters added after the last inspection may not be reflected on a form completed before the work. Your wind mitigation form has a shelf life.

OIR also encourages consumers to verify that their inspector “is authorized under Section 627.711(2)(a)” of the Florida Statutes.

What this does not tell you

None of this is a promise of a saving. Whether a credit applies, and how much, depends on the home, the insurer and what the inspector can verify. For how deductibles on a Florida homeowners policy can work, see our posts on the hurricane deductible percentage and the separate roof deductible.

One question to ask

It is a short question: which mitigation form does my insurer have on file, and what date is on it? Our Florida homeowners insurance page explains how we approach this line.
