Florida Commercial Insurance Renewal Notice: The Date to Calendar Is Not January 1

If your business policy renews on January 1, the Florida commercial insurance renewal notice rules put the date to watch around November 17. Under Fla. Stat. §627.4133, your insurer owes you at least 45 days’ advance written notice of nonrenewal or of the renewal premium. That 45-day notice window is where your leverage sits.

What you are owed: 45 days

Under Fla. Stat. §627.4133(1), the insurer must give the first-named insured at least 45 days’ advance written notice of nonrenewal or of the renewal premium, if the insured furnished the necessary information before the expiration date.

That condition is worth reading twice. The notice right is tied to your own responsiveness. If your carrier asked for something and it has not been sent, the clock you are counting on may not run the way you expect.

Mid-term cancellation has its own clocks

Cancellation has separate timing under the same statute:

  • Cancellation or termination for reasons other than nonpayment generally takes at least 45 days’ notice.
  • Nonpayment takes 10 days’ notice, with the reason stated.
  • Within the first 60 days of a new policy, 20 days’ notice applies for reasons other than nonpayment.

A quiet change of carrier is still a notice event

Section 627.4133(8) requires at least 45 days’ notice before a commercial lines policy is transferred to an affiliated insurer. If the name on your renewal paperwork is different, that is a reason to ask questions, not to skim.

The first question is about time

When a business owner asks what to question before re-signing, the first question is not “what is the number.” It is “when did the number arrive, and what do I still owe the underwriter.”

Forty-five days is a real window to review the account and talk to a broker about options. Two weeks is not. Most owners spend the window waiting.

What to check this week

  1. Have your renewal terms arrived? Note the date they came in.
  2. Does your carrier have everything it asked for? The 45-day right is conditioned on it.
  3. Who is the issuing insurer? Compare it with your expiring policy.
  4. Read the renewal declarations page. Our post on how to read a declarations page walks through what to check.
