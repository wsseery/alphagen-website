Florida Insurance Market 2026: What the State’s Own Report Says, and What It Leaves Out

“The Florida insurance market 2026 is improving” is a claim. Here is the state’s own version of it, with the caveat that matters most to commercial owners.

What the report says

The figures below come from the Florida Office of Insurance Regulation’s Property Insurance Stability Report of January 1, 2026.

  • Capacity. “Seventeen new companies have been approved to write residential property policies in Florida since the reforms.”
  • Rate filings. For residential policies effective in 2024 or later, 39 companies requested a rate decrease and 100 requested a 0 percent rate change.
  • Reinsurance. Florida Hurricane Catastrophe Fund rates approved in June 2025 decreased for participating insurers by a statewide average of -9.51%, and the 2025 Annual Reinsurance Data Call reports “a decrease of -0.46% in risk-adjusted reinsurance costs” from 2024.

The caveat: the report is residential

That report is residential. It contains no commercial-lines results. Its only commercial reference is to the reporting scope of the claims data call. So none of the figures above is a statement about commercial property or liability results.

Why it matters to a commercial policyholder

The reinsurance easing underneath is real and measured. It does not automatically arrive on your renewal. Your own loss history, class and construction still decide that. The report is a useful read on the state of the market; it is not a forecast of what any one commercial policy will do.

What to do with it

  • Compare your last commercial renewal with the direction those numbers suggest. Did it move the same way?
  • Look at your own loss history, class and construction, the factors that still decide your renewal.
  • Read the wording on your commercial property policy, not just the headline.
