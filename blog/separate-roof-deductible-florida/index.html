Separate Roof Deductible Florida: The Page the Legislature Made Bold

A separate roof deductible in Florida comes with a page that exists only because the legislature concluded people were not reading the fine print. If a policy carries one, the law says how that page must look and where it must sit.

What the statute requires

If a personal lines residential policy carries a separate roof deductible, Fla. Stat. §627.701(10)(e) requires a disclosure on the page immediately behind the declarations page. No other policy language may appear on that page, and the type must be boldfaced and no smaller than 18 point.

The statute dictates the substance of the wording too. The page must say that the policyholder is electing coverage containing a separate deductible for roof losses, that this may result in high out-of-pocket expenses, and that they should discuss it with their insurance agent.

Why that level of detail is informative

That level of prescription is unusual. A legislature does not specify placement, exclusivity of the page and a minimum point size unless the ordinary disclosure was reliably failing to reach anyone.

A trade, not a trap

A separate roof deductible is not a trap. It is a trade. It applies a different deductible structure to roof losses specifically, distinct from the rest of the policy and distinct from the hurricane deductible, and it exists because roof losses drive a large share of claims. Some homeowners will take that trade knowingly. The statute’s concern is the word knowingly.

The dollar figure must be findable

The same subsection requires the actual dollar value of the roof deductible to appear on the declarations page or the premium renewal notice, at issuance and at renewal. The figure is meant to be findable rather than buried in a schedule. If you need a refresher on the front page itself, see how to read a declarations page.

What to check

  1. Look one page behind the declarations page. If that bold page is there, the policy has a separate roof deductible.
  2. Find the dollar figure on the declarations page or the renewal notice.
  3. Decide whether the trade still makes sense for you, and ask your agent if anything is unclear.

Whether a given loss falls inside your policy is a question for that policy’s actual terms, forms and endorsements.
