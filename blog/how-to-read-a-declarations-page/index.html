How to Read a Declarations Page: The Summary Is Not the Contract

Knowing how to read a declarations page takes about four minutes, and it starts with one fact: the declarations page is the summary of your policy, not the policy itself. It tells you the who, what, where and how much. It does not tell you what is excluded.

What a declarations page is

The NAIC glossary of insurance terms defines declarations as “policy statements regarding the applicant and property covered.” In plain terms: who is insured, what is insured, where, for what period, and for how much.

What it will not tell you

What is excluded does not live on the declarations page. It lives in the policy form, and in the endorsements. The NAIC defines an endorsement as “an amendment or rider to a policy adjusting the coverages and taking precedence over the general contract.”

Read that last phrase again: taking precedence. An endorsement can change what the declarations page appears to promise. The page you were sent summarises; the form and endorsement numbers printed on it are what point you to the documents that decide a claim.

What to check, in order

  1. Named insured spelling. The name should match your legal entity or your own name exactly.
  2. Policy period. The start and end dates.
  3. Each limit. Read every limit listed, not just the first.
  4. Each deductible. Note which coverage each one applies to.
  5. The list of form and endorsement numbers. Ask for a copy of any you have never seen, and read them.

That last step is the one most people skip. If you do not recognise a form or endorsement number, you have not yet read part of your contract.

Why renewal is a good time to do it

A renewal declarations page is a chance to compare what you have against what you expected. If something has changed from last term, or if you cannot tell, ask. Whether a given loss falls inside your policy is a question for that policy’s actual terms, forms and endorsements.
