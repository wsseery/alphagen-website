D&O Insurance for a Small Company: What Three Directors Should Check

D&O insurance for a small company is easy to dismiss as something only big boards need. A company with three directors is still a board: it signs contracts, borrows money, hires people and makes calls that someone may later disagree with.

Three directors is still a board

It is natural to think of directors and officers as a feature of large corporations. In practice, any company with directors has people who make decisions on its behalf. Those decisions can be questioned later, sometimes by people who were not in the room when they were made. The size of the board does not change that.

What D&O liability insurance is written for

Directors and officers (D&O) liability insurance is written for claims alleging that the people running a company got a management decision wrong. The decisions in question are the everyday ones: signing a contract, taking on a loan, hiring someone, or choosing a direction for the business. Whether the board is small or large, the people who made the decision are the ones a claim tends to name.

It is a different policy from general liability and E&O

D&O is not a substitute for the other business policies, and they are not a substitute for it:

  • General liability is written for injury and property damage. Our plain-English guide to general liability covers what it is and isn’t.
  • E&O (errors and omissions) is written for the service you sell.
  • D&O is written for management decisions made by the people running the company.

Because the three are written for different things, having one in place does not tell you whether you have the others. If you are also thinking about limits above your liability policies, see when a commercial umbrella makes sense.

What to check on a D&O quote

  • Who counts as an insured person. Current directors only, or former directors and officers too? People leave boards, and a decision made while they served can be questioned after they have gone.
  • Who the policy protects. Does it protect the company itself, or only the individuals? The answer shapes who the policy is there for when a claim is made.
  • What the exclusions remove. Read them as closely as the insuring agreement, because they set the boundary of what the policy is written for.

Those three questions will not settle everything, but they are a fair place to start reading a quote. The wording you are offered matters more than any summary of it, including this one.
