What to Fix After Hurricane Season in Florida: Use the December–May Window

Hurricane season ends on November 30. So what to fix after hurricane season in Florida? Book the work the statute already pays you for, and book it now, while the calendar is on your side.

The window is real

The Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 to November 30 (NOAA). December through May is the only stretch when inspectors and mitigation contractors have capacity. After that, they are busy again. A roof or opening project that waits until spring competes with everyone else’s.

For scale on the season now closing: NOAA’s 2026 Atlantic outlook called for 8-14 named storms, 3-6 hurricanes and 1-3 major hurricanes, at 70% confidence.

The discount is required, not discretionary

Florida law provides that a rate filing for residential property insurance “must include actuarially reasonable discounts, credits” for properties with “fixtures or construction techniques demonstrated to reduce the amount of loss in a windstorm” (Fla. Stat. §627.0629(1)). Whether a credit applies, and how much, depends on the property and the insurer. This post makes no promise of a saving.

The statute names the categories

The statute points to features that “enhance wind uplift prevention, roof strength, roof covering performance,” “roof-to-wall strength, wall-to-floor-to-foundation strength, opening protection, and window, door, and skylight strength.” That list is a useful checklist for the off-season: roof, connections, openings.

For the form that documents those features, and why the date on it matters, see our post on the Florida wind mitigation inspection form. This post is the timing angle; that one covers the paperwork.

Commercial owners

The discount provision above is written for residential property. The underwriting logic (roof, openings, connections) is the same conversation on a commercial building, and it is the same six months. Our commercial property insurance page explains how we approach that line, and homeowners can start at Florida homeowners insurance.

One question to ask

Before December ends, ask: which of these categories is the weakest on my building, and who can look at it before the busy season?
