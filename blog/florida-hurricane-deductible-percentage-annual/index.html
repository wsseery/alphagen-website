Florida Hurricane Deductible: A Percentage That Applies Annually

Most homeowners can tell you their deductible. Far fewer can tell you their Florida hurricane deductible, and those are usually two different numbers that behave in two different ways.

Two deductibles, two behaviours

The everyday deductible is typically a flat dollar amount. The hurricane deductible is typically a percentage of the dwelling limit. Florida requires insurers to offer hurricane deductibles equal to $500, 2 percent, 5 percent and 10 percent of the policy dwelling limits, with an exception once dwelling limits reach $250,000 or more, where the $500 option need not be offered (Fla. Stat. §627.701).

Why a percentage is harder to hold in your head

Percentages are harder to picture than dollars, which is the whole problem. Here is a hypothetical, not a quote: on a home insured for $600,000, a 5 percent hurricane deductible is $30,000 before the policy responds. That is arithmetic on an illustrative example, not a price, a rate or a statement about any real policy — but it is the arithmetic that decides whether a roof claim is worth filing.

How the hurricane deductible resets

The part that surprises even careful people is the reset. In Florida the hurricane deductible applies on an annual basis, to covered hurricane losses during the calendar year, rather than starting fresh with each storm. The statute’s wording is that it “shall apply on an annual basis to all covered hurricane losses that occur during the calendar year” (Fla. Stat. §627.701(5)(a)1).

Where there was an earlier hurricane loss in the same year, the statute sets out how the deductible is applied to a later one. In a season with two landfalls, that distinction is the difference between one large retention and two. How it applies to a particular loss depends on the statute and your policy’s actual terms.

What to check on your declarations page

What to check is not complicated. Your declarations page states both deductibles. Look for:

  • Your everyday deductible, usually a flat dollar amount.
  • Your hurricane deductible, usually shown as a percentage.

The useful question is which percentage you chose, and whether you chose it or inherited it at renewal. If you are not sure, ask your agent to walk you through it.
