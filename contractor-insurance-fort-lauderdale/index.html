Contractor Insurance in Fort Lauderdale

Trade contractors in Broward County get asked for the same three things, usually in the same week: a certificate that satisfies the general contractor’s exhibit, proof of workers’ compensation that matches the job, and limits someone else chose. AlphaGen writes Florida commercial direct and shops trade accounts across markets so the certificate you hand over is the one the contract actually asks for.

What a trade account in Broward usually has to answer for

General liability. The foundation, and the policy the contract exhibit is almost always describing. What the additional-insured wording says, whether it reads primary and non-contributory, and whether a completed-operations extension is in place are the three things that decide whether your certificate clears.

Workers’ compensation. Florida counts construction differently from every other industry: the obligation starts at one employee, not four. (Fla. Stat. §440.02(17)(b)) And a contractor who sublets work is liable for securing compensation for a subcontractor’s employees unless that subcontractor has secured it. (Fla. Stat. §440.10(1)(b))

Commercial auto. Florida sets liability minimums for commercial motor vehicles by gross vehicle weight — $50,000 per occurrence at 26,000 to 34,999 pounds, rising to $300,000 at 44,000 pounds and above. (Fla. Stat. §627.7415)

Excess and umbrella. Usually a contract trigger before it is an exposure one. Florida requires an indemnification provision in a construction contract to carry a monetary limitation bearing a reasonable commercial relationship to the contract, and not less than $1 million per occurrence where a property owner is the indemnified party. (Fla. Stat. §725.06(1))

Tools and equipment. Rarely on the auto policy, rarely on the general liability policy. Usually its own form, and usually the thing nobody has read.

If the work involves building, clearing, filling, excavating, or substantially improving a structure or the appearance of land, Florida treats it as construction for workers’ compensation purposes — whatever the sign on the truck says.

What to have ready before you bid

  1. The insurance exhibit from the contract, including the limits and the additional-insured wording it demands.
  2. Current certificates for every subcontractor on the job, with the expiration dates checked rather than the file.
  3. Payroll by trade classification, and the officer exemption certificates for anyone treated as exempt.
  4. Gross vehicle weight for each vehicle in the fleet, and the described use shown on your current declarations page.
  5. A list of what the tools and equipment on each truck are worth.
