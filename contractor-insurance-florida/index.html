Contractor insurance in Florida

Contractors usually carry several policies at once: general liability for the work, workers' compensation for the crew, inland marine for tools and equipment, and builder's risk while a project is under way. General contractors and owners often decide which ones you need through the contract.

AlphaGen Insurance Agency is an independent agency in Boca Raton, licensed in Florida, that compares several insurers for Florida businesses and households.

Who it's for

General contractors, specialty trades (roofing, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, carpentry), handymen and remodelers working in Florida.

Subcontractors asked by a general contractor to carry specific limits and endorsements before they can start.

What it can include

  • General liability — including completed operations, which matters long after a job is finished.
  • Workers' compensation — required at Florida's construction-industry thresholds.
  • Inland marine — tools and equipment that travel to the job.
  • Additional insured and waiver endorsements — the items contracts most often require.

Coverage depends on the policy. Nothing on this page describes what a specific policy will do: eligibility, underwriting and the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy actually issued decide that.

What insurers ask for

To quote this line, an underwriter needs a picture of the risk. These are the items most insurers ask for. Not every insurer asks for every item, and some ask for more.

Business basics

  • Legal name, entity type and FEIN
  • Years in business and a description of operations
  • Locations and, where relevant, the class code that best describes the business — the class code sets which rating base and rate an underwriter uses

Exposure

  • Revenue and payroll by trade classification
  • The cost of subcontracted work, and your split of residential, commercial and new construction — new residential construction is priced very differently from commercial remodeling

Line-specific

  • Sample contracts, including hold-harmless and additional insured requirements — contracts show whether you are taking on liability that belongs to someone else
  • Certificates of insurance from your subcontractors
  • A list of recent completed projects and current backlog
  • Your safety manual and any fleet safety program

History

  • Three to five years of currently valued loss runs from your current and prior insurers — they show claims the insurer would otherwise only learn about later
  • Your current insurer, current premium and expiration date

Application forms: most insurers start from the industry-standard ACORD 125 Commercial Insurance Application, ACORD 126 Commercial General Liability Section and, for workers' compensation, the ACORD 130 FL; many also use their own application or supplement.

Having your loss runs, inspections and current declarations ready usually speeds up a quote, because the underwriter can price from documents instead of waiting on follow-up questions.

Questions we hear

Why does every general contractor ask for a certificate?

Because a contractor can be held responsible for an uninsured subcontractor's injuries and damage. The certificate shows them your coverage before you start.

What is additional insured status?

An endorsement that extends some of your policy's protection to another party, such as a general contractor or owner, usually because the contract requires it.

Does my policy cover faulty work?

Not as such. Liability policies generally respond to damage your work causes, not the cost of redoing the work itself. The exact answer depends on the policy wording.

How much does contractor insurance cost in Florida?

What drives the price is the business itself: what it does, its revenue or payroll, its location, its claims history, the limits and deductibles chosen, and the insurer's appetite for that class of business.
