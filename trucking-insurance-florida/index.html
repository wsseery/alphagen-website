Trucking insurance in Florida

Trucking insurance is commercial auto written for motor carriers: businesses that haul freight for others, or their own goods, in heavy trucks. Beyond auto liability it usually adds cargo and physical damage coverage and, for carriers with federal operating authority, the filings that prove the coverage is in place.

Who it's for

For-hire motor carriers with their own operating authority, from a single owner-operator to a fleet.

Owner-operators leased to another carrier, who often need physical damage and non-trucking liability rather than primary liability.

Businesses that haul their own goods in heavy trucks, such as building-supply, landscape and equipment companies.

What it can include

  • Primary auto liability — injuries and damage to others caused by your trucks.
  • Motor truck cargo — the freight you haul, up to the cargo limit chosen.
  • Physical damage — your tractors, trucks and trailers.
  • Non-trucking liability — for an owner-operator's truck when it is not being used in a motor carrier's business.
  • Trailer interchange and truckers general liability — someone else's trailer in your care, and liability from operations away from the truck.

Coverage depends on the policy. Nothing on this page describes what a specific policy will do: eligibility, underwriting and the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy actually issued decide that.

What insurers ask for

To quote this line, an underwriter needs a picture of the risk. These are the items most insurers ask for. Not every insurer asks for every item, and some ask for more.

Business basics

  • Legal name, entity type and FEIN
  • Years in business and a description of operations
  • Locations and, where relevant, the class code that best describes the business — the class code sets which rating base and rate an underwriter uses

Operating authority

  • Your USDOT number and, if you have one, your MC number, and how long you have operated under that authority — insurers read your safety record in the federal regulator's public data from that number
  • Whether you haul for hire, run as an owner-operator leased to another carrier, or haul your own goods

Operations

  • What you haul, roughly what share of your loads each commodity makes up, and the cargo limit you need — commodity drives both the cargo and the liability price
  • The radius you run and the states you operate in
  • Owner-operators or other carriers you use, and any trailer interchange agreements

Equipment and drivers

  • A schedule of tractors, trucks and trailers with year, make, VIN and value
  • A driver list with license numbers, dates of birth, years of commercial driving experience and motor vehicle records
  • Your driver hiring standards and safety program, including any dash cameras or telematics

History

  • Three to five years of currently valued loss runs from your current and prior insurers — they show claims the insurer would otherwise only learn about later
  • Your current insurer, current premium and expiration date

Application forms: most insurers start from the industry-standard ACORD 125 Commercial Insurance Application, ACORD 129 Vehicle Schedule and ACORD 132 Truckers/Motor Carriers Section, with the ACORD 143 Transportation Section for cargo; many truck insurers use their own application; many also use their own application or supplement.

Having your loss runs, inspections and current declarations ready usually speeds up a quote, because the underwriter can price from documents instead of waiting on follow-up questions.

Questions we hear

What are the federal insurance filings?

A carrier applying for operating authority from the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration needs its insurer to file proof of liability coverage, usually on the BMC-91 or BMC-91X filing, before the authority is granted. Policies for for-hire and interstate carriers also carry the MCS-90 endorsement.

What is non-trucking liability?

Coverage for an owner-operator leased to a motor carrier, for times the truck is used outside that carrier's business. While the truck is under the carrier's dispatch, the carrier's own liability policy usually responds instead.

Is it harder to insure a new trucking company?

Usually. Fewer insurers write carriers with new authority, so driver experience, the equipment and what you haul carry more weight in the quote.

Why does what I haul matter?

Cargo and liability are both priced by commodity. Hazardous materials, refrigerated loads and high-value freight raise the exposure, and some insurers will not write certain commodities at all.

How much does trucking insurance cost in Florida?

What drives the price is the vehicles and the people driving them: the type and value of each vehicle, how it is used and how far it travels, where it is garaged, the drivers' records, the business's claims history, and the limits and deductibles chosen.
