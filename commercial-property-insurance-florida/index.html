Commercial property insurance in Florida

Commercial property insurance responds to physical loss or damage to the buildings you own and the property your business uses, subject to the causes of loss the policy lists and the ones it excludes. In Florida, how wind and hurricane are handled is usually the biggest question.

Who it's for

Business owners who own their building, landlords of commercial space, and tenants who need to insure improvements, equipment and inventory.

Lenders usually require it on a financed building, with the lender named on the policy.

What it can include

  • Building — the structure, if you own it or your lease makes you responsible for it.
  • Business personal property — furniture, fixtures, equipment and stock.
  • Business income and extra expense — on many forms, lost income and extra costs after a covered loss.
  • Wind and hurricane — included, written with a separate deductible, or excluded and placed elsewhere, depending on the insurer and the location.

Coverage depends on the policy. Nothing on this page describes what a specific policy will do: eligibility, underwriting and the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy actually issued decide that.

What insurers ask for

To quote this line, an underwriter needs a picture of the risk. These are the items most insurers ask for. Not every insurer asks for every item, and some ask for more.

Business basics

  • Legal name, entity type and FEIN
  • Years in business and a description of operations
  • Locations and, where relevant, the class code that best describes the business — the class code sets which rating base and rate an underwriter uses

Property details

  • Construction type, year built, square footage and number of stories
  • Roof type and age, and updates to the roof, wiring, plumbing and heating and cooling
  • Fire protection: sprinklers, alarms and the local protection class
  • Wind-mitigation features such as roof-to-wall connections and opening protection — in Florida these drive much of the wind premium, and Florida requires insurers to offer credits for them
  • Flood zone
  • Building value, business personal property value and business income limit, or a statement of values for several locations

History

  • Three to five years of currently valued loss runs from your current and prior insurers — they show claims the insurer would otherwise only learn about later
  • Your current insurer, current premium and expiration date

Application forms: most insurers start from the industry-standard ACORD 125 Commercial Insurance Application and ACORD 140 Property Section, with an ACORD 139 Statement of Values for several locations; many also use their own application or supplement.

Having your loss runs, inspections and current declarations ready usually speeds up a quote, because the underwriter can price from documents instead of waiting on follow-up questions.

Questions we hear

What is a named-storm or hurricane deductible?

A separate deductible that applies to wind damage from a named storm or hurricane. In Florida it is often a percentage of the insured value rather than a flat dollar amount, so read it before renewal.

Replacement cost or actual cash value?

Replacement cost pays to repair or replace without deducting for depreciation; actual cash value deducts it. The choice changes both the premium and what a claim pays.

Is flood included?

Usually not. Flood is commonly excluded from commercial property forms and placed on a separate commercial flood policy.

How much does commercial property insurance cost in Florida?

What drives the price is the business itself: what it does, its revenue or payroll, its location, its claims history, the limits and deductibles chosen, and the insurer's appetite for that class of business.
