Homeowners insurance in Florida

A homeowners policy insures the home you live in, your belongings and your personal liability. In Florida the answers that matter most are about wind: the hurricane deductible, the roof, and the wind-mitigation features the inspector records.

AlphaGen Insurance Agency is an independent agency in Boca Raton, licensed in Florida, that compares several insurers for Florida businesses and households.

Who it's for

Owners of a single-family home they live in, from a starter home to a high-value or coastal property.

Buyers who need proof of coverage before closing, since lenders require it.

What it can include

  • Dwelling and other structures — the house, plus detached structures such as a fence or shed.
  • Personal property — your belongings, with special limits on certain items such as jewelry.
  • Loss of use — extra living costs while the home cannot be lived in after a covered loss.
  • Personal liability — claims that you injured someone or damaged their property.

Coverage depends on the policy. Nothing on this page describes what a specific policy will do: eligibility, underwriting and the terms, conditions and exclusions of the policy actually issued decide that.

What insurers ask for

To quote this line, an underwriter needs a picture of the risk. These are the items most insurers ask for. Not every insurer asks for every item, and some ask for more.

The home

  • Year built, construction, square footage and number of stories
  • Roof type, age and condition, and the year of the last full replacement — Florida law (Florida Statutes section 627.7011) limits refusing or non-renewing a policy on roof age alone for newer roofs, and an inspection can show remaining life on older roofs
  • Updates to wiring, plumbing and heating and cooling
  • Pools, docks and other features
  • For high-value homes, a replacement-cost estimate, scheduled jewelry, art or collections, and central-station alarms

Florida documents

  • A wind-mitigation inspection on Florida's OIR-B1-1802 form — the features it records earn premium credits Florida requires insurers to offer
  • A four-point inspection for older homes, and a roof inspection where asked — many insurers ask for one on older homes, and some will not quote without it
  • Your flood zone and, if you have one, an elevation certificate

History

  • Claims in the past five years, your current insurer and your renewal date

Application forms: most insurers start from the industry-standard ACORD 80 Homeowner Application; many also use their own application or supplement.

Having your loss runs, inspections and current declarations ready usually speeds up a quote, because the underwriter can price from documents instead of waiting on follow-up questions.

Questions we hear

Is flood part of homeowners insurance?

No. Flood is excluded from standard homeowners policies and is written on a separate flood policy.

What is a hurricane deductible?

A separate deductible for hurricane wind damage, often a percentage of the dwelling limit. In Florida it generally applies once per calendar season rather than once per storm.

Why does the insurer want a wind-mitigation inspection?

Florida requires insurers to offer premium discounts for features that reduce wind damage, such as roof-to-wall connections and opening protection. The inspection documents those features.

How much does homeowners insurance cost in Florida?

What drives the price is the property itself: its location and flood zone, its age, roof and construction, its wind-mitigation features, the coverage limits and deductibles chosen, and its claims history.
